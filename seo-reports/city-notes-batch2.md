## Rules that apply to all 5 cities

- **Outdoor burning (state):** Brush from on-site land clearing may be burned on the property it came from, by the owner or someone the owner authorizes [B2]. Where that falls under the general conditions [B3]:
  - outside city limits, unless the city allows it;
  - downwind of, or at least 300 ft from, neighboring structures with people, livestock or sensitive plants, unless the neighbor gives written approval;
  - start no earlier than 1 hour after sunrise and finish no later than 1 hour before sunset;
  - predicted wind of 6–23 mph;
  - someone there the whole time the fire is active.

  The rule has separate clauses for counties in and out of air-quality nonattainment areas [B2]. Which clause applies to each county: [UNVERIFIED].
- **Oak wilt:** Texas A&M Forest Service says to avoid pruning or wounding oaks February through June, paint every fresh wound any time of year, and lists land or cedar clearing as a source of wounds [B4].
- **Golden-cheeked warbler (endangered):** Found in the Edwards Plateau and locally north to Palo Pinto County. Nests only in mixed Ashe juniper–oak woodland in ravines and canyons [B5]. Make no claims about when a permit is needed; send people to USFWS.
- **Edwards Aquifer contributing zone (Hays and Travis counties are covered):** "Clearing of vegetation without soil disturbance" and most agricultural activities are excluded. Clearing or excavation that alters the ground or its storm runoff is regulated [B6]. Whether a tract is in the zone needs a TCEQ map check [UNVERIFIED]. Don't say whether mulching counts as "without soil disturbance" [UNVERIFIED].
- **Cedar:** Ashe juniper dies when cut, but seed in the soil means land is due for another treatment in 7–8 years (TPWD) [B7].
- **Unincorporated county land:** I found no county tree ordinance for any of these counties. I didn't find an official source saying there isn't one [UNVERIFIED].

## 1. Dripping Springs

**County:** Hays, on US 290, 25 miles west of Austin [B8]. Distance: **~38 mi** from Bertram. Drive probably 60–75 min [UNVERIFIED].

**Terrain and geology:** The Balcones Escarpment divides Hays County: hilly, tree-covered ranch country in the northwest three-quarters, farm plains in the southeast [B9]. Elevation 600 to over 1,400 ft, rising to the west [B9]. The city's own draft ordinance calls the landscape "thin-soiled rock formations" with frequent droughts [B10]. Early farms were in the Little Barton and Onion Creek valleys [B8].
**Soil:** County soils run from thin limestone to black, waxy loam [B9]. Brackett soil (type location in Hays County) is 5–20 in. deep over weathered limestone, mainly Glen Rose [B11]. How much of Dripping Springs is Brackett: [UNVERIFIED].
**Vegetation:** Live oak, cedar, pecan and mesquite are native to the county [B9]. Elm [site].
**Land uses:** In 2002, 67% of county farm and ranch land was pasture [B9]. Ranches, hunting property and rural estates [site].
**Rules:**
- **City tree ordinance (Article 28.06), adopted March 5, 2024** [B12]. Reported at adoption: no removal of heritage trees, meaning 18 in. or larger of specific species [B12].
- **March 2025 update (staff-recommended draft)** [B10]. **Final adoption and current text: [UNVERIFIED].** In that draft:
  - Individual residential lots and projects under five homes are covered **inside city limits only**. Larger residential and commercial projects are covered in the city **and the ETJ**.
  - Heritage = 18 in.+ trunk for all species (8 in.+ for small natives such as Texas persimmon, redbud and mountain laurel). Cedar isn't on its list of unprotected species.
  - Outside a development project, an owner may remove any tree except a heritage tree.
  - So a large cedar may count as heritage in city limits. **Ask the city before telling anyone cedar is exempt** [UNVERIFIED].
- **Hays County:** Bans cover outdoor burning. With no ban, county rules and state limits apply, and the Fire Marshal's number is 512-393-7339 [B13]. Whether notice or a permit is required for land-clearing burns: [UNVERIFIED].
- **Warbler:** Hays County has a federal permit landowners can use as one option for mitigating habitat removal [UNVERIFIED: search excerpt of B14].

**Nearby:** Driftwood (~6 mi), Bee Cave (~11), Lakeway (~13), Wimberley (~15). Pedernales Falls State Park, about 13 mi northwest [B15]. US 290 and Hamilton Pool Road [site].

## 3. Burnet

**Terrain and geology:** The county is gentle to broken hills on the northeast edge of the Hill Country, with fertile valleys in the east and rolling prairie to the north. 700–1,700 ft [B25]. The county produces granite, limestone, industrial sand and graphite [B25]. The Llano-Burnet area looks mountainous next to the flatter plateau to the south (USGS) [B26, search excerpt].
**Soil:** Shallow rocky soils, sandy loams and clay loams tied to limestone and granite [site]. One Burnet County plat near Oatmeal lists Brackett, Doss and Krum soils [B27, search excerpt]; that site isn't in Burnet itself.

## 4. Lakeway

**County:** Travis, on Lake Travis and SH/RM 620, 5 miles west of Mansfield Dam [B33]. Distance: **~27 mi** from Bertram. Drive probably 45–60 min [UNVERIFIED].

**Terrain and geology:** Travis County sits on the eastern edge of the Edwards Plateau, split by the Balcones Escarpment; land west of it is drier [B34]. Hills, steep slopes and rocky drainages along the lake [site].
**Soil:** Shallow limestone clay loams [site]. No soil survey pulled [UNVERIFIED].
**Vegetation:** County: juniper, mesquite, oak, redbud and pecan, depending on which side of the escarpment [B34]. Cedar, live oak, red oak, persimmon, prickly pear [site].
**Land uses:** Began on a 2,700-acre ranch; incorporated in 1974 [B33]. Large residential lots, lakefront and estates [site].
**Rules (the strictest of the 5). From Ordinance 2025-10-06-01, approved Oct 6, 2025 [B35]; check for later changes:**
- **Cedar (*Juniperus ashei*) is listed as an unprotected tree.** Unprotected trees don't need a removal permit.
- **Protected:** any hardwood 12 in.+ on private property. **Heritage:** 24 in.+ (except unprotected species).
- **Homes:** a single-family home's non-heritage trees don't need a permit under listed conditions. A heritage tree does. The ordinance lists the conditions with "or"; read it before quoting.
- **Non-residential and multifamily land clearing (city and ETJ):** no site clearing without a subdivision, site development or land clearing permit; erosion and tree protection must be in place first.
- **Vacant single-family lots:** whether clearing needs a permit: [UNVERIFIED]. Ask Building and Development Services.
- **Oaks:** no pruning **Feb 1 – June 30**; seal every cut within 10 minutes [B36]. Fines of $500–$1,000 a day are possible; oak wilt–susceptible oaks may not be planted [B37]. Red oaks killed by oak wilt must be cut and the wood destroyed within three days [B38].
- **Burning:** Travis County's page showed no ban and said to contact your local ESD about permits [B39]; its date is unclear. City burning rule: [UNVERIFIED].
