## Rules that apply to all 7 cities

- **Outdoor burning (state):** TCEQ bans outdoor burning statewide except in listed cases. Brush from on-site land clearing is one of those cases, with conditions [S2][S3]. County burn bans and city ordinances can be stricter, and you have to follow them too [S3]. Only material from that property may be burned on site [S3]. I didn't get the full text of the land-clearing conditions in 30 TAC §111.209 (setbacks, wind, timing), so read the rule before quoting specifics [UNVERIFIED].
- **County burn bans:** A commissioners court can ban outdoor burning in unincorporated areas when it finds a public safety hazard (Local Gov't Code §352.081) [S4]. Texas A&M Forest Service only *displays* the bans. Counties set and lift them [S1].
- **Oak wilt:** Texas A&M Forest Service says to avoid pruning or wounding oaks **February through June**, and to paint every fresh oak wound right away, all year. It lists "land or 'cedar' clearing" as a source of wounds [S5].
- **City tree mitigation fees (state limit):** A city can't charge a tree mitigation fee for removing a tree under 10 in. diameter (measured 4.5 ft up) on an existing one- or two-family home that is the owner's residence. Where a city does charge a fee, it must offer a planting credit [S6].
- **Unincorporated county land:** I found no county tree ordinance for Burnet, Williamson, Gonzales, or Caldwell County. As far as I know, Texas counties generally can't adopt tree ordinances, but I didn't find an official source that says so [UNVERIFIED].
- **Golden-cheeked warbler (endangered):** The bird nests in mature Ashe juniper–oak woodland. Balcones Canyonlands NWR covers parts of Burnet, Travis, and Williamson counties [S7]. Clearing that kind of habitat has involved federal permits in the past [S8]. I found **no current USFWS landowner guidance** on when a permit is needed [UNVERIFIED]. Ask the USFWS Austin Ecological Services Field Office before you put anything about this on the site.

## 1. Liberty Hill

**County:** Williamson [site][S9]. Distance: **~11 mi** from Bertram (straight line). Drive is probably ~15–20 min via SH 29 [UNVERIFIED].

**Terrain and geology:** Rolling Hill Country with ranchland and creek valleys [site]. The town sits between the North and South Forks of the San Gabriel River [S9]. Limestone and limey subsoil are common, especially west of the Balcones Fault Zone, with deeper clay farther east [site]. Hand-cut native limestone buildings date back to 1871 [S10].
**Soil:** Varies. Clay-rich and limestone-derived, with shallow rocky soil in places [site]. No NRCS soil survey pulled yet [UNVERIFIED].
**Vegetation:** Ashe juniper (cedar), live oak, mesquite, native grasses, mixed brush [site].
**Land uses:** Ranches and rural acreage [site]. Fast-growing subdivisions and new homesites [UNVERIFIED: no source pulled].
**Rules:**
- **City of Liberty Hill:** The city has a heritage-tree permit. It covers removing, modifying, or treating a tree that is *already designated* heritage, and the Planning Director decides within 10 business days [S11]. A 2021 *draft* ordinance would allow removing up to 40% of trees under 19 in.; hardwoods 19 in. and up would need P&Z approval; hackberry, mesquite, and Ashe juniper would be exempt [S12]. **Adoption and current numbers: [UNVERIFIED].**
- **Williamson County (outside city limits):** The county Fire Marshal doesn't issue burn permits. Bans run up to 90 days, and violating one is a Class C misdemeanor (up to $500) [S13][S14].
- **Edwards Aquifer:** Parts of Williamson County fall in TCEQ's Edwards Aquifer zones (30 TAC 213) [S15]. A single-family homeowner building their own house is exempt from the protection-plan requirement if they stay at or under 20% impervious cover [S16]. Whether any given Liberty Hill tract is in the recharge zone needs a map check [UNVERIFIED].

**Nearby:** Leander (~7 mi), Georgetown (~12 mi, 14 mi east on SH 29 per [S17]), Jonestown (~13 mi). Liberty Hill International Sculpture Park, which started with a 1976 symposium [S18].

**Your work within ~15 mi:**
- Case study: *Rock Removal in Liberty Hill* (4.1 ac, rock and dead trees removed, 12 loads of debris hauled, 10 loads of dirt spread, native seed) [site]
- Nearby: Leander wedding venue (1 ac, ~7 mi); both Bertram jobs (~11 mi); Jonestown (1.2 ac, ~13 mi) [site]
- Reviews: **Holton Garrison** and **Preston Jones**, both rock removal in Liberty Hill; **Dylan Gavit** (Bertram, ~11 mi) [site]

**Draft FAQs**

The city has a heritage-tree permit process [S11], and its tree rules may cover large hardwoods [S12; UNVERIFIED as adopted]. A 2021 draft exempted Ashe juniper (cedar) [S12]. Outside city limits, we haven't found a county tree permit [UNVERIFIED].

## 2. Georgetown

**County:** Williamson (county seat) [site][S19]. Distance: **~22 mi** from Bertram. Drive is probably ~30–35 min via SH 29 [UNVERIFIED].

**Terrain and geology:** Sits on the Balcones Escarpment / east edge of the Edwards Plateau. Edwards Limestone outcrops show up in northwest Georgetown [S20]. Layers dip east-southeast and are broken up by Balcones faults [S21]. Lake Georgetown is the dammed North Fork of the San Gabriel River [S20]. Limestone has historically been quarried there [site].
**Soil:** Deep Blackland Prairie clay on the east side, shallower limestone clay loams to the west [site].
**Vegetation:** Live oak, pecan, cedar elm, mesquite, hackberry; Ashe juniper in western areas [site].
**Land uses:** Farms, ranches, acreage, homesites, residential and commercial development [site].
**Rules (the strictest of the 7 cities):**
- **City of Georgetown tree ordinance (UDC Ch. 8, amended Sept 2025)** [S22]. It covers the city **and its ETJ** [S22]. Removing or pruning a Heritage or Protected tree needs a permit (UDC 3.23, 3.24, 8.02) [S23].
- **Heritage tree:** 26 in. DBH or larger, for listed species such as live oak, post oak, cedar elm, pecan, and southern magnolia. Council can also designate other trees [S24]. Heritage tree work needs P&Z approval and a Texas-registered arborist [S23].
- **Protected-tree retention** (not residential subdivisions): keep 20% if there are 11+ trees/acre, 30% if 10 or fewer [S25]. Critical root zone = 1 ft of radius per inch of DBH [S22].
- **Protected-tree size threshold: [UNVERIFIED].** I couldn't find the number. Check UDC §8.02 or email trees@georgetowntexas.gov [S24].
- **Edwards Aquifer:** Northwest Georgetown is in the TCEQ Edwards zones. Projects there file Edwards Aquifer applications or exception requests [S15][S20].
- County burn rules: same as Liberty Hill [S13].

**Nearby:** Liberty Hill (~12 mi), Leander (~12 mi), Round Rock, Lake Georgetown, San Gabriel River [site][S20].

**Your work within ~15 mi:**
- Gallery photos: **5-acre cedar clearing in Georgetown** (before/after, cedar cleared from under oaks) [site, `app/gallery/page.tsx`]. Not a case study yet.
- Nearby: Liberty Hill rock removal (~12 mi), Leander venue (~12 mi) [site]
- Reviews: no review names Georgetown. Holton Garrison and Preston Jones are in Liberty Hill (~12 mi) [site]

**Draft FAQs**
1. *Do I need a permit to remove trees in Georgetown?* Draft: Often, yes. Inside the city and its ETJ, removing a Heritage or Protected tree needs a city permit [S22][S23]. Heritage trees are 26 in.+ of listed species such as live oak and pecan [S24]. Ask the city's Urban Forester before clearing (trees@georgetowntexas.gov) [S24].
2. *Can you clear cedar without hurting my oaks?* Draft: That was the goal on our 5-acre Georgetown job, where we cleared cedar from under oaks [site]. Texas A&M recommends no oak wounding Feb–June [S5].
3. *Is my land over the Edwards Aquifer, and does that change anything?* Draft: Parts of Georgetown are in TCEQ's Edwards Aquifer zones [S15][S20]. Building your own single-family home under 20% impervious cover is exempt from the protection-plan filing [S16]. Check the TCEQ map for your tract.
